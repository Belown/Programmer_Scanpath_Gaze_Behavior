# Model Analysis

1. **Random Effects**: Random intercept SDs for exp_a = 0.01238 and exp_b = 0.01090; residual SD = 0.04047. Adjusted ICC = 0.142, meaning that ~14% of the total variance in score is attributable to stable between-participant differences (small-to-moderate individual differences).
2. **Nakagawa R²**: Marginal R² = 0.0021; Conditional R² = 0.1443. Fixed effects (expertise_mean and expertise_diff) explain ~0.2% of the variance; participant-level random effects account for an additional ~14%.
3. **Post-hoc inference**:
   - expertise_mean: β ≈ +0.00361, SE = 0.00228, z = 1.59, p = .112. No reliable association with score.
   - expertise_diff: β ≈ +0.00085, SE = 0.00116, z = 0.73, p = .465. No evidence of an association with score.
  Conclusion: Neither expertise_mean nor expertise_diff predicts score. The fixed effects contribute negligibly to explained variance, and the dominant source of systematic variability arises from between-participant differences captured by the random effects rather than from expertise-related predictors.
