# Summary

## 1 Rendering effects dominate performance

Across models, render_a shows large, robust effects on score, whereas render_b shows weak or no reliable differences.

- r1 consistently outperforms r2 and r3 for render_a, with large effect sizes and p < .0001.
- r2 and r3 are typically indistinguishable.
- Render_b contrasts are mostly non-significant.
- Rendering explains a substantial share of variance (Marginal R² ≈ 0.35).

## 2 Expertise × Rendering interaction adds little beyond rendering

Including expertise_a and its interaction with render_a increases explained variance modestly, but the dominant driver remains rendering.

- r1 > r2 and r1 > r3 remain highly significant.
- Beginner vs. Intermediate shows only borderline evidence (Δ ≈ +0.025, p ≈ .050).
- r2 ≈ r3 remains non-significant.

## 3 Mean and difference in expertise are weak predictors

Models using continuous expertise metrics (expertise_mean, expertise_diff) explain virtually none of the variance.

- Marginal R² < 1%.
- expertise_mean shows a trend-level negative slope (p ≈ .09).
- expertise_diff is clearly non-significant.
- Most explainable structure comes from random effects (participants).

## 4 PairType has no reliable effect

Categorical PairType (BB, BI, II) does not predict score.

- All pairwise contrasts are non-significant after Tukey adjustment.
- Marginal R² < 1%.
- Variance is dominated by between-participant differences.

## Overall conclusions

- Rendering (render_a) is the primary and most reliable determinant of score.
- Expertise effects are weak, inconsistent, or marginal at best.
- Pair composition (BB/BI/II) has no detectable impact.
- Between-participant variability is substantial across all models (ICC ≈ 0.11–0.27).
- Fixed effects unrelated to rendering contribute negligibly to explained variance.
Performance is driven overwhelmingly by rendering quality (especially render_a), not by expertise level or pair composition.
