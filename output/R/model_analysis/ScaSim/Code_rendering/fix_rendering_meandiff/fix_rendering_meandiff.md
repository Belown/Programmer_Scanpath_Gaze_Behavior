# Model Analysis

1. **Random Effects**:Random intercept SDs for exp_a = 0.01521 and exp_b = 0.01989; residual SD = 0.04909. Adjusted ICC = 0.206, meaning that ~21% of the total variance in score is attributable to stable between-participant differences (moderate individual differences).
2. **Nakagawa R²**: Marginal R² = 0.0239; Conditional R² = 0.2254. Fixed effects (expertise_mean and expertise_diff) explain ~2.4% of the variance; participant-level random effects account for an additional ~20%.
3. **Post-hoc inference**:
   - expertise_mean: β ≈ −0.0249, SE = 0.00951, z = −2.62, p = .0089. Statistically significant negative association with score: higher average expertise is associated with slightly lower scores.
   - expertise_diff: β ≈ +0.0010, SE = 0.00528, z = 0.18, p = .8570. No evidence of an association with score.
Conclusion: There is a small but statistically reliable negative effect of expertise_mean on score, whereas expertise_diff has no detectable impact. Fixed effects contribute only modestly to explained variance, and most systematic variability arises from between-participant differences captured by the random effects rather than from expertise asymmetry.
